THE CHALLENGE

You cannot modernise
what you cannot see.

Cryptography is woven through applications, services, certificates, protocols, and software dependencies. A safe transition begins with understanding where it exists—and what depends on it.

01 / VISIBILITY

Cryptographic blind spots

Cryptographic functions may live in direct or transitive dependencies, legacy systems, vendor components, and configurations that are difficult to inventory.

02 / PRIORITY

Unclear migration priorities

A list of algorithms is not a migration plan. Teams need context about data lifetime, system criticality, exposure, and the effort required to change.

03 / VALIDATION

Change without disruption

New cryptographic mechanisms must be evaluated for interoperability, performance, compatibility, and operational impact before broad deployment.

i

The engineering challenge: move from fragmented knowledge to evidence-based decisions—without assuming every system can be migrated in the same way.

UNDERSTANDING THE URGENCY

The risk is not just
about Q-Day.

Q-Day is an informal term for the point when a sufficiently capable quantum computer could break certain widely used public-key cryptographic systems. Its timing remains uncertain—but data collected today may need protection for years to come.

Harvest Now, Decrypt Later

An adversary may store encrypted data today in the hope of decrypting it in the future. This is especially relevant when information must remain confidential for a long time.

The appropriate response depends on the data, its confidentiality lifetime, current protections, and the organisation's threat model.

A PLANNING HEURISTICX + Y > Z

Mosca's Equation

A way to reason about whether the time needed to protect data and migrate systems may exceed the estimated time available.

X
Data confidentiality lifetimeHow long must the information remain protected?
Y
Migration durationHow long could the transition take?
Z
Estimated time to a relevant quantum computerAn uncertain estimate, not a known deadline.
ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE12 years + 5 years > 10 years

If data needs 12 years of confidentiality and migration may take 5 years, a 10-year assumed horizon suggests urgency under those assumptions.

Mosca's equation is a planning heuristic, not a prediction of when Q-Day will occur.

A MIGRATION LIFECYCLE

From discovery to
informed transition.

PQC migration is a programme of engineering work—not a single switch. These stages describe a practical framework we are studying and refining.

01
VISIBILITY

Discover

Identify cryptographic algorithms, libraries, protocols, certificates, configurations, and relevant dependencies across selected systems.

Output: evidence-backed findings
02
STRUCTURE

Inventory & map

Organise discovered assets and relationships. A Cryptographic Bill of Materials (CBOM) can help represent cryptographic components in a structured format.

Output: inventory and dependency context
03
RISK CONTEXT

Assess & prioritise

Consider algorithm exposure, data lifetime, business criticality, system dependencies, and migration complexity to inform priorities.

Output: documented assumptions and priorities
04
ENGINEERING

Evaluate & test

Assess suitable standards-based PQC and hybrid approaches. Test compatibility, interoperability, performance, and security in controlled environments.

Output: reproducible test evidence
05
TRANSITION

Plan, migrate & monitor

Use phased deployment, validation, and ongoing review. Build crypto-agility so future cryptographic changes can be managed deliberately.

Output: a maintainable migration process
This lifecycle is a synthesis of common migration practices, not a claim that every organisation must follow one identical sequence.

DESIGNED FOR CHANGE

Crypto-agility is not
a feature. It's a discipline.

Crypto-agility is the ability to change cryptographic algorithms, parameters, or implementations without redesigning an entire system each time. It depends on architecture, governance, testing, and operational readiness.
